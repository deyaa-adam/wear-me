Predicting insulin resistance from wearables and routine blood tests

We studied the WEAR-ME clinical study, which blends continuous smartwatch data with standard lab biomarkers to flag insulin resistance years before type 2 diabetes appears, without an invasive clamp test.

Why it matters

Insulin resistance (IR) develops silently, often long before HbA1c changes. The median HbA1c in the cohort was a normal 5.4%, so glycemic screening alone would miss many participants.

Clamp test

The gold standard, but highly invasive and limited to research settings.

OGTT

Takes 2+ hours and needs several blood draws.

HOMA-IR

Used as the ground truth here: below 1.5 insulin sensitive, 1.5 to 2.9 impaired, above 2.9 insulin resistant.

How the framework works

Three input streams feed a gradient-boosting model that predicts HOMA-IR, then classifies IR with a threshold.

STEP 1

Wearable signals

Resting heart rate, HRV, daily steps, sleep stages and circadian patterns from a Fitbit or Pixel Watch.

STEP 2

Wearable Foundation Model

A WFM pre-trained on 1.4 million people turns raw time series into embeddings that capture non-linear patterns.

STEP 3

Fusion with labs

Embeddings are reduced with PCA and combined with demographics, fasting glucose and the lipid panel.

STEP 4

Explain and communicate

SHAP explains each prediction, and an LLM agent turns it into patient-friendly guidance.
